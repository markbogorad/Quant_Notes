# Real Options
- Acquisitions of assets are highly dependent on real options
	- Highly dependent on current market conditions -> a reason why [[Oil Options]] market is so large
## 4 Real Options
- **Option to produce**
	$$max(P_{oil}-C_{prod},0)$$
	- Long call option on [[Crude Oil]]
	- Strike: is cost of production
	- [[OPEC+]] intentionally maintain sparse capacity to be able to exercise this option at higher prices
	- When production is cheap, the strike is very low - they effectively always exercise the option (eg: Russia)
	- [[Shale Oil]] producers can stop production when oil gets cheap
		- Production and prices are inversely correlated because producers realize they're going to drop the price down and won't want to sell at a lower price
	- Business of producing oil is like a call option on oil
	- Either business moves ahead and profits cover costs of production and exploration or it doesn't and oil stays unrefined and in the ground
	- Volatile oil price makes the real option valuable

- **Option to transport**
	$$max(EU Oil)-(US Oil))-Freight,0)$$
	- Ships and pipelines
	- Spread: price in location X - price in location Y
	- Strike: is cost of shipping (tradeable number)
	- In other commodities
		- Electricity grids
		- Nat gas pipelines
	- Owning oil transport infrastructure is like a [[Locational Spread]]
		- Price in the location you are sending to should cover transport costs
		- Option on location

- **Option to store (time)**
	$$max(P(T_2)-P(T_1))-Cost of Storage,0)$$
	- Put option on a spread - if market is in contango, buy physical and sell futures, otherwise do nothing
	- Spread: price at time t - price at time T
	- Strike: Cost of storage
	- The largest participants in energy trading is storage hedgers (Glencore, Trafigura)


- **Option to refine**
	$$max(\Sigma \text{Refined Product} (i) - \Sigma \text{Oil} (j) - \text{Cost of Refining},0)$$
	- Spread: Option on a basket of these refined products
		- Can be basket on a basket (lots of oils and lots of refined products)
		- Oil companies analyze which products to buy and which to produce daily
	- Strike: cost of refining
	- If heating oil exceeds cost of refining, then sell heating oil and buy crude oil
	- Owning a refinery that processes oil is like an option on the spread between refined products and crude inputs
	- Crack spread
