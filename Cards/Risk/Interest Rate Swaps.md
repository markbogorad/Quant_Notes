# Interest Rate Swaps
- Exchanging one stream of payments for another
- Most common: vanilla swaps
	- Fixed payments to floating SOFR
	- SOFR has a yield curve as well - rate determined off this
- Swap market makers 
	- Banks with strong credit rating - offer fixed and floating sides
- Counterparties
	- Corporation, bank, investor
- Purpose: 
	- for counterparty to match their balance sheet to the nature of their liabilities (fixed or floating liabilites)
	- Speculation: these contracts require little upfront capital - better alternative to directly investing into a t-bill
- Swaps are quoted by the their fixed rate, or the **swap spread** between SOFR and a local govt bond yield
- Carry [[Interest Rate Risk]] and [[Credit Risk]]

## How the swap works
- At the time a swap contract is put into place, it is typically considered “at the money,” meaning that the total value of fixed interest rate cash flows over the life of the swap is exactly equal to the expected value of floating interest rate cash flows
-  If the forward SOFR curve, or floating-rate curve, is correct, the 2.5% he receives will initially be better than the current floating 1% SOFR rate, but after some time, his fixed 2.5% will be lower than the floating rate.
